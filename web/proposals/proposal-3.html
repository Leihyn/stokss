03 / The clock

The volume does not wait for the bell

If trading stopped when pricing stopped, the window would be harmless. It does not. Most of the volume in these pools arrives when the underlying has no live price, and the hours nobody can price are the more volatile ones.

04 / The exit

You sign it. Nobody else touches the position.

Closing Bell is a warning system with one button on the end of it. It watches the session clock against your open liquidity, tells you what is about to sit through unpriceable hours, and builds the exit transaction for you to sign yourself.

01 — Watch

Read the position, read the clock

Your open liquidity is resolved pool by pool, and each underlying is matched against its real session calendar rather than a generic one.

02 — Warn

Before the bell, not after

At T−60 minutes you are told which positions are about to spend hours in a pool whose price cannot move to meet reality.

03 — Sign

The transaction is yours

The exit is simulated, shown to you in full, and signed by your wallet. No keeper approval, no delegated withdrawal, no custody at any point.

04 — Clear

Out, or in on purpose

You leave before the bell, or you stay knowing exactly what you are staying in. Both are decisions. Neither is an accident.

Read the method Non-custodial. The protocol never holds a key and never moves a position on its own.

05 / Method & states

Where every number came from

No figure on this page is illustrative. Each one is read back from a measurement or a live feed, and anything derived is labelled as derived.

Provenance

Position counts are per-pool reads at a single capture while the US equity session was closed: 2,777 positions across 76 pools, $16.2M of pool liquidity covered. Session-volume and volatility splits are measured over the same sample. The Pyth price, its publish age and the issuer’s maxOrderFiatValue are live account reads.

Derived, and marked as such: the 199-position tail (total minus the 18 listed pools), the 34.6% extended-hours share (70.9 minus 36.3), and the 20.9% relative volatility gap.

How the crowd is drawn

One mark per position, packed into columns inside its own pool block, blocks ordered by size and hung from one shared rule. No physics, no randomness, no pointer tracking — the same input always draws the same picture.

On narrow screens nothing is dropped or aggregated: the row count rises from 34 to 64, which re-flows the identical 2,777 marks into a taller, narrower crowd. Per-block labels are withdrawn below 640px, where they would be sub-pixel, and replaced by tap and the ledger.

States & access

Four feed states ship, and all four can be seen in place using the control in the hero: live, loading, empty and error. Loading draws the real cluster geometry as flat blocks with a determinate count, never a spinner and never a shimmer.

The chart is one tab stop; arrow keys move between blocks and Enter jumps to that pool’s ledger row. Severity is always a dot, a label and a colour together. Controls are 44px. Under prefers-reduced-motion the crowd renders complete and still.

Closing Bell

A warning system for tokenized-equity liquidity providers. Most tokenized stock trades when nobody can price it; this tells you when you are one of them, and hands you an exit you sign yourself.
